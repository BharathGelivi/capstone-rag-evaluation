Question
Under the BSA, when are facts relevant because they are consistent or inconsistent with the opinions of experts?
X-RAG's Answer
Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), a fact that is not otherwise relevant becomes relevant **when it either supports or is inconsistent with the opinion of an expert**, provided that the expert’s opinion itself is relevant to the issue at hand. This rule is set out in **Section 40** of the BSA: > **40. Facts, not otherwise relevant, are relevant if they support or are inconsistent with the opinions of experts, when such opinions are relevant.** The section is illustrated with two examples: 1. **Poisoning case** – The fact that other persons who were poisoned by the same poison exhibited certain symptoms (which experts affirm or deny to be the symptoms of that poison) is relevant. 2. **Harbour obstruction** – The fact that other harbours similarly situated, but without a particular sea‑wall, began to be obstructed at about the same time is relevant to the opinion about whether the sea‑wall caused the obstruction. Thus, under the BSA, facts are admitted on the basis of their **consistency or inconsistency with expert opinion** whenever the expert’s opinion is pertinent to the matter in issue. *Sources: Section 40 and its illustrations in the retrieved context (Chunk 6: 25a797b4-63c0-45a3-9cc8-c711c4ed193b).*
