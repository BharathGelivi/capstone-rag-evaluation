The Case
Question
Under the BSA, are facts inconsistent with a fact in issue, or that make the existence of a fact in issue highly probable or improbable, relevant even if not otherwise relevant?
X-RAG's Answer
Yes. Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), facts that are **inconsistent with a fact in issue** or that **make the existence or non‑existence of a fact in issue highly probable or improbable** are deemed relevant **even when they would not otherwise be relevant**. This is expressly stated in Section 9 of the BSA: > “Facts not otherwise relevant are relevant— > (1) if they are inconsistent with any fact in issue or relevant fact; > (2) if by themselves or in connection with other facts they make the existence or non‑existence of any fact in issue or relevant fact highly probable or improbable.”【1f44cb03-d12a-41ef-940b-49a1848dd6ec】 Thus, such facts are admissible to prove or disprove a fact in issue despite lacking any other connection to the case.
