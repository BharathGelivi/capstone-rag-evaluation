The Case
Question
Under the BSA, are judgments, orders, or decrees (other than those covered by sections 34-36) generally relevant in other proceedings?
X-RAG's Answer
Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), judgments, orders, or decrees that are **not** covered by sections 34‑36 are **generally irrelevant** in other proceedings. Section 37 expressly states: > “Judgments or orders or decrees, other than those mentioned in sections 34, 35 and 36, are irrelevant, **unless the existence of such judgment, order or decree is a fact in issue, or is relevant under some other provision of this Adhiniyam**.”【38b29cd4-3798-4a9c-9612-53418e213360】 Thus, such judgments are only admissible when: - Their existence itself is a fact in issue in the case, or - Another provision of the BSA makes them relevant (e.g., they pertain to a matter of public nature under section 36, or they are needed to prove fraud, collusion, or lack of jurisdiction under sections 38‑39). Outside of these exceptions, they are not considered relevant evidence.
