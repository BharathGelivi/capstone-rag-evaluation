Question
What must a police officer be satisfied of, in addition to the existence of a reasonable complaint or suspicion, before arresting a person without a warrant for a cognizable offence punishable with imprisonment up to seven years, per BNSS section 35?
X-RAG's Answer
Under BNSS § 35(1)(b), a police officer may arrest without a warrant a person against whom a reasonable complaint, credible information, or reasonable suspicion exists for a cognizable offence punishable with imprisonment up to seven years **only if** the officer is also satisfied that the arrest is **necessary** for one (or more) of the following purposes: - to prevent the person from committing any further offence; - for the proper investigation of the offence; - to prevent the person from causing the evidence of the offence to disappear or to tamper with such evidence; - to prevent the person from making any inducement, threat, or promise to anyone acquainted with the facts of the case so as to dissuade them from disclosing those facts to the court or police; - to ensure that the person’s presence in court whenever required can be ensured. The officer must record in writing the reasons for being satisfied of this necessity (or, if arrest is not required, the reasons for not arresting)【5e158157-c2be-4da0-a502-178c54007990】.
