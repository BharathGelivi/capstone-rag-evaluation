Question
What legal consequence follows if someone deliberately sets fire to a dwelling-house after being instigated to do so by another person who has a guilty intention, where the person who set the fire lacked the mental capacity to know the act was wrong?
X-RAG's Answer
The person who actually set the fire (B) is **not guilty of any offence** because, due to unsoundness of mind, they were incapable of knowing the nature of the act or that it was wrong. However, the instigator (A), who acted with a guilty intention, is **guilty of abetting the offence of setting fire to a dwelling‑house** and is liable to the punishment prescribed for that offence. This follows directly from the illustration in the retrieved context: > “A instigates B to set fire to a dwelling‑house. B, in consequence of his unsoundness of mind, being incapable of knowing the nature of the act, or that he is doing what is wrong or contrary to law, sets fire to the house in consequence of A’s instigation. B has committed no offence, but A is guilty of abetting the offence of setting fire to a dwelling‑house, and is liable to the punishment provided for that offence.”【63cb2c24-3b1a-4f5f-bdce-52797e73b527】.
