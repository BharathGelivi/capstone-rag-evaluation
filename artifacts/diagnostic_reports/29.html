The Case
Question
Under the BSA, what must be true of a document for the Court to presume it is the Official Gazette, a newspaper, or a journal, and thereby presume its genuineness?
X-RAG's Answer
Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), the Court will presume a document to be the Official Gazette, a newspaper, or a journal—and therefore presume its genuineness—**only if**: 1. The document is **kept substantially in the form required by law**; and 2. It is **produced from proper custody** (i.e., it is in the place where, and looked after by the person with whom, the document is required to be kept, or its origin is shown to be legitimate or probable under the circumstances). This is set out in Section 80 of the BSA, which states that the Court shall presume the genuineness of every document purporting to be the Official Gazette, a newspaper, or a journal **if such document is kept substantially in the form required by law and is produced from proper custody**【a895513d-dd0a-4ee4-9d0c-af9a2d72375f】. The accompanying explanation clarifies what constitutes “proper custody.”
