Under what circumstances does the right of private defence of the body NOT extend to voluntarily causing the assailant's death?
X-RAG's Answer
The right of private defence of the body **does not** extend to voluntarily causing the assailant’s death in the following situations: 1. **The offence is not one of the categories listed in § 38** – i.e., it is not: - an assault that reasonably causes apprehension of death, - an assault that reasonably causes apprehension of grievous hurt, - an assault with the intention of committing rape, - an assault with the intention of gratifying unnatural lust, or - an assault with the intention of kidnapping or abducting. *[§ 39: “If the offence be not of any of the descriptions specified in section 38, the right of private defence of the body does not extend to the voluntary causing of death …”]*【90f8747a-7f40-4ae3-b114-2b216129f432】 2. **The act is done by a public servant (or under his direction) in good faith under colour of office and does not reasonably cause apprehension of death or grievous hurt.** *[§ 37(1)(a)‑(b): no right of private defence against such acts]*【69db30c9-72ad-48eb-b310-9e3137806fa2】 3. **There is time to have recourse to the protection of the public authorities.** *[§ 37(1)(c)]*【69db30c9-72ad-48eb-b310-9e3137806fa2】 4. **The defence would inflict more harm than is necessary for the purpose of defence.** *[§ 37(2): “The right of private defence in no case extends to the inflicting of more harm than it is necessary to inflict for the purpose of defence.”]*【6f639d07-62fa-48e0-a811-5492ff51880e】 In short, the right to cause death is limited to those assaults that raise a reasonable apprehension of death or grievous hurt (or the specific serious intents listed in § 38); outside those bounds, the defender may only use force sufficient to cause non‑lethal harm.

45. A person abets the doing of a thing, who—
(a) instigates any person to do that thing; or
(b) engages with one or more other person or persons in any conspiracy for the
doing of that thing, if an act or illegal omission takes place in pursuance of that
conspiracy, and in order to the doing of that thing; or
(c) intentionally aids, by any act or illegal omission, the doing of that thing.
Explanation 1.—A person who, by wilful misrepresentation, or by wilful concealment
of a material fact which he is bound to disclose, voluntarily causes or procures, or attempts
to cause or procure, a thing to be done, is said to instigate the doing of that thing.
Illustration.
A, a public officer, is authorised by a warrant from a Court to apprehend Z. B, knowing
that fact and also that C is not Z, wilfully represents toAthat C is Z, and thereby intentionally
causes A to apprehend C. Here B abets by instigation the apprehension of C.
Explanation 2.—Whoever, either prior to or at the time of the commission of an act,
does anything in order to facilitate the commission of that act, and thereby facilitates the
commission thereof, is said to aid the doing of that act.
46. A person abets an offence, who abets either the commission of an offence, or the
commission of an act which would be an offence, if committed by a person capable by law of
committing an offence with the same intention or knowledge as that of the abettor.
